What do these mean?

Creek level

Measured in the creek by OneShoreline's gauges, on creeks that have them. Watch: the creek is rising toward flooding. Warning: flooding is likely or happening.

These are creek measurements, separate from National Weather Service flood alerts. Follow whichever is more serious.

Rain in the hills above

Shown where creek levels aren't measured for alerts yet. It tells you how unusual the rain upstream is for that spot: Heavy is heavier than a typical storm, Very heavy happens only every few years, Severe is rare.

Rain is not the same as flooding. Soaked ground makes flooding more likely with less rain.

Tide

On creeks near the Bay, high tide slows drainage. A dark teal note means heavy rain and high tide are happening together.

Staff view — not linked from the public page. Open public view. Official creek level comes from the creek dashboards and always takes priority. Chance in any year = annual exceedance probability (AEP) of the heaviest rain at any of the creek's gauges. Severe rain needs two gauges agreeing. Rain + tide = very heavy or severe rain within 2 hours of high tide.
