Official creek level comes from the creek dashboards and always takes priority. Rain is shown for every creek: on dashboard creeks it is an early heads-up, elsewhere it is the only indicator. Chance in any year is the annual exceedance probability (AEP) of the heaviest rain at any of the creek's gauges. Severe rain needs at least two gauges agreeing.

Rain is not the same as flooding. A creek can flood with less rain when the ground is already soaked, and a heavy downpour does not always cause flooding. Where an official creek level is shown, follow it.

“10% chance in any year” means rain this heavy is expected about once a decade on average — but it can happen two years in a row.

Rain thresholds: NOAA Atlas 14 Vol. 6 v2 point precipitation frequency estimates (partial-duration series) at each gauge; AEP = 1 − e−1/ARI. Never walk or drive through moving water. In an emergency, call 911.
